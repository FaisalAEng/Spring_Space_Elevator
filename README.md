# Spring-Powered Elevator Prototype
**Pole-Climbing “Space Elevator” Mechanism**

![Spring-Powered Elevator Prototype](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FaisalAEng/portfolio-website/refs/heads/main/assets/images/projects/spring_space_elevator.PNG)

**Engineering Design Competition – First Place** (8–10 teams)  

---

## Project Overview
Designed, fabricated, and tested a **purely mechanical climbing mechanism** capable of ascending a 7-foot (84 in) vertical steel pole using a **constant-force coil spring** as the sole power source.  

The project emphasized:
- Rapid prototyping under tight academic timelines  
- Limited access to CNC and 3D printing resources  
- Strict material and mass constraints  

The goal was to deliver a **lightweight, mechanically simple, and reliable system** using iterative design, testing, and mechanical optimization.

**Performance**
- **Climb Height:** 62 in (≈81% of total pole height)  
- **Time to Climb:** ~0.6 s  
- **Total Mass:** 169 g
- **Competition Result:** Only team to surpass the halfway mark; the highest climb overall

---

## My Contributions
- Developed mechanical concept and overall system layout  
- Designed and modeled key components in **CAD (SolidWorks)**, optimizing geometry for strength, weight, and manufacturability  
- Prepared **CAM toolpaths** for CNC-machined MDF components; participated in machining and CNC coding  
- Conducted testing, iterative design refinements, and performance tuning  
- Documented design iterations, testing results, and lessons learned for future improvements  

---

## Outcome
The prototype achieved the **fastest and highest climb**, successfully ascending 62in. of the 84in. pole, securing **first place overall**.  

This project demonstrates practical engineering skills in:
- Mechanical system design and actuation  
- Rapid prototyping and iterative testing  
- Manufacturing under material and time constraints  
- Delivering reliable performance with minimal iteration opportunities  

These skills are directly transferable to **mechanical design, robotics, and constrained-environment systems**.
